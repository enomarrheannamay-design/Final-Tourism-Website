Our Story

History of MIMAROPA

MIMAROPA is known as Region IV-B of the Philippines. The name MIMAROPA comes from the names of its provinces: Mindoro, Marinduque, Romblon, and Palawan. These provinces are made up of islands and coastal communities with different local traditions and histories.

The region has developed through its communities, local industries, agriculture, fishing, tourism, and cultural traditions. Its islands have become known for their natural attractions while local communities continue to preserve their customs and heritage.

Mindoro is divided into two provinces, Occidental Mindoro and Oriental Mindoro, which makes five provinces in all. Each one has its own languages, stories, and ways of life, shaped by the sea, the mountains, and the people who have lived here for generations.

Today, MIMAROPA is recognized as an important tourism region because of its beaches, islands, natural attractions, cultural traditions, and historical places.

A Journey Through Time

From ancient caves to a modern tourism region.

🗿
Ancient Times

The First Islanders

The Tabon Caves in Palawan are among the earliest known sites of human presence in the Philippines, where remains of "Tabon Man" were found. A burial jar from Manunggul Cave, about 2,800 years old, shows how early communities honored their dead.

⛵
Early Communities

Fishing, Farming & Trade

Island communities fished, farmed, and traded across the seas. Indigenous peoples such as the Mangyan of Mindoro and the Tagbanua, Batak, and Palaw'an of Palawan built traditions that are still alive today.

⛪
Spanish Period

Churches, Towns & Forts

Churches, towns, and stone forts were built across the islands. Fort Santa Isabel in Taytay, Palawan, and Fort San Andres in Romblon still stand as reminders of this era.

🏥
Early 1900s

The Story of Culion

Culion Island became a major center for the care and treatment of people with leprosy. Its story is now told at the Culion Museum and Archives.

🕊️
World War II

Wartime Memories

The islands were affected by the war. Lubang Island is remembered for Hiroo Onoda, a Japanese soldier who kept hiding there until 1974.

🗺️
2002

MIMAROPA Is Formed

The region was created as Region IV-B and took the name MIMAROPA from its provinces, giving the islands a shared regional identity.

🌅
Today

A Region of Islands

Beaches, reefs, festivals, and local crafts draw visitors from around the world, while communities work to protect their nature and traditions.

The People

People & Communities

The many voices that make up MIMAROPA.

🌾

The Mangyan

📍 Mindoro

The Mangyan are the indigenous peoples of Mindoro, with groups such as the Iraya, Alangan, Tadyawan, Tau-buid, Buhid, Bangon, Hanunuo, and Ratagnon. The Hanunuo Mangyan still use their own script to write ambahan, traditional poetic verses.

🌊

Tagbanua, Batak & Palaw'an

📍 Palawan

These indigenous peoples of Palawan have long lived in harmony with its forests, rivers, and seas, and keep their own languages, rituals, and crafts.

🏝️

The Cuyonon

📍 Palawan

Originally from the Cuyo Islands, the Cuyonon brought their language and traditions to many parts of Palawan.

🗿

The Romblomanon

📍 Romblon

Romblon's island communities speak languages such as Romblomanon, Asi, and Onhan, and are known for their marble artistry and seafaring heritage.

⛪

The Marinduqueños

📍 Marinduque

Known for their deep faith and Holy Week traditions, the people of Marinduque keep their customs alive through community celebrations.

🗣️

Many Languages

📍 All Provinces

Along with Filipino and English, you will hear Tagalog, Cuyonon, Hanunuo, Romblomanon, and many other local languages across the region.

Celebrations

Festivals

Color, music, faith, and community.

🎭 Holy Week

Moriones Festival

📍 Marinduque

Participants wear handmade wooden masks and Roman soldier costumes during Holy Week, retelling the legend of Longinus through street plays and processions.

🥁 Street Dancing

Biniray Festival

📍 Romblon

A lively celebration of Romblon's culture, featuring colorful costumes, music, and street dancing by the community.

🎪 Usually June

Baragatan Festival

📍 Palawan

A showcase of Palawan's towns, where local products, food, and traditions are displayed in Puerto Princesa.

🎉 All Year

Town Fiestas

📍 Every Province

Local town fiestas and cultural celebrations bring families together with food, music, and prayer.

Handmade

Traditional Crafts

Skills passed down from hand to hand.

🗿

Marble Craftsmanship

Romblon is often called the marble capital of the Philippines, and its artisans carve everything from small souvenirs to large sculptures.

🧵

Traditional Weaving

Banton Island is known for its traditional cloth, woven in patterns that reflect local heritage.

🧺

Mangyan Basketry

Mangyan communities weave baskets and other items from natural materials such as rattan and nito vines.

🎭

Moriones Masks

In Marinduque, artisans carve and paint wooden masks for the Moriones Festival, each one with its own character.

🐚

Island Handicrafts

Handmade local products, from shell and bamboo crafts to woven souvenirs, are sold in markets across the islands.
